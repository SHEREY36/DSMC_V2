# Figure captions

Definitions kept out of the axis labels to keep the panels legible.
Reduced velocities: c = v/sqrt(2 Ttr/m), w = omega/sqrt(2 Trot/I); u-hat is the
body axis. theta^H = Trot/Ttr at the attractor. tau counts collisions per
particle. Error bars are the standard error over ten independent realizations
and are smaller than the markers except where shown.

**Fig. 1 — The HCS attractor is reached long before the retained window.**
Ensemble-mean Trot/Ttr against tau for the two bracketing initial conditions
(theta_0 = 0.025 or 0.225, and 1.5), five realizations each, at three
representative coordinates. Logarithmic in both axes so the transient is
visible. Dashed line: tau = 130, the slowest approach measured anywhere on the
grid. Shading: the retained sampling window tau in [500,1500]. The branches are
merged by tau ~ 20-60 at every coordinate.

**Fig. 2 — Equipartition breaking and the smooth-sphere limit.**
(a) theta^H against aspect ratio. The gas approaches equipartition as rods
lengthen and departs sharply as they approach spheres, because AR sets the
tangential lever arm and therefore the rotational-translational coupling; AR->1
is the smooth-sphere limit, not the rough one. At alpha = 1 the exact elastic
block returns theta^H = 1 to within 7e-4. (b) The same data as theta^H - 1
against AR - 1; slopes are fitted on the three smallest aspect ratios.

**Fig. 3 — Elongation shields dissipation.**
(a) alpha_eff = sqrt(1 - 3 zeta*), the normal restitution a smooth sphere gas
would need to cool at the measured rate, against the prescribed alpha. Dashed
line: smooth spheres, alpha_eff = alpha. (b) The excess alpha_eff - alpha
against aspect ratio, showing saturation near AR ~ 2. zeta* is obtained from
the energy-weighted per-collision loss and reproduces Haff's law fitted to
freely cooling runs to within 0.02-2.3 %.

**Fig. 4 — Fourth-order cumulants along both arms of the cross design.**
(a-c) against aspect ratio at three restitutions; (d-f) against restitution at
three aspect ratios. Dotted curve in (d): the smooth inelastic-hard-sphere
Sonine coefficient (van Noije & Ernst 1998). The measured a20 crosses that
reference near alpha ~ 0.8, being suppressed below it at strong dissipation and
enhanced above it at weak dissipation. a11 changes sign between AR = 1.5 and
2.0 for alpha >= 0.8. All magnitudes stay within +-0.11, about twenty times
smaller than the O(1) cumulants reported for constant-beta rough disks.

**Fig. 5 — Orientational observables with no rough-sphere analogue.**
(a) A_cu = <(c.u-hat)^2 - c^2/3>; positive values mean rods preferentially
translate along their own axis. The three restitutions collapse:
A_cu = 0.00734 (AR - 1) fits through the origin with 5-10 % residuals and a
12 % spread in the prefactor over 0.5 <= alpha <= 0.95, while A_cu vanishes
identically at alpha = 1. (b) A_cw = <(c.w)^2 - c^2 w^2/3> (solid) against the
value forced by axis geometry alone, -A_cu/2 (dotted), since a linear body's
angular velocity lies in the plane normal to u-hat. (c) The dynamical share,
1 + A_cu/2 A_cw: most of the velocity-spin anti-alignment is a genuine
correlation at near-sphere shapes, and geometry takes over only at large AR.

**Fig. 6 — Every fitted tail exponent against its exact-Maxwellian null.**
Dashed line and band: mean +- 3 sigma of the same fit applied to the alpha = 1
exact elastic arm, which is Maxwellian by construction. An exp(-gamma_c c) fit
to exp(-c^2) over c in [3,5] returns 6.60 analytically, against a measured null
of 6.554 +- 0.065. Measured exponents leave the band only for alpha <~ 0.6, and
the departure shrinks with aspect ratio. The gamma_cw channel (c) fails its
negative control: the exact Maxwellian itself passes the asymptotic power-law
gate at every aspect ratio, so no algebraic tail can be claimed from it.
